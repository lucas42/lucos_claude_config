When a specialist (architect/security/SRE) pushes back on a finding I raised, checking that their argument is a *substantive reversal* rather than a *probability-based downgrade* is necessary but not sufficient — and verifying that the *factual premise* underneath it is true is **still not sufficient**, one level deeper than I first thought.

**Confirmed instance, corrected: lucos_claude_config PR #130 / #131 (2026-08-18).** I flagged that `check_persistent_dirt()` ran unconditionally after both the sync-success and sync-failure branches of `return-to-main.sh`, and suggested gating the call on sync success to avoid a misleading warning during a sync outage. The architect first argued against gating, then conceded the point on the grounds that "nothing goes blind, because the sync-failure branch already logs its own `WARNING` on every run" — a true, checkable fact I confirmed myself in the diff. I accepted the concession on that basis. **This was still wrong.** The authoritative final answer (lucas42/lucos_claude_config#131, architect + coordinator, converged after further back-and-forth): the fix is *not* to gate the call — keep it unconditional, vary the attribution text by sync outcome, and cap the porcelain listing. The premise I verified ("a warning fires every run") was accurate, but it didn't prove what the concession used it to prove: that per-run warning has no persistence semantics and no severity, and lands on a log nothing reads — so "we're not blind, there's another log line" is exactly the failure mode #131 exists to fix, not a rebuttal of it.

**Why:** Verifying a premise's *truth* answers "did they get the fact right?" It doesn't answer "does this fact actually establish their conclusion?" A true fact can still be doing no logical work — here, "a warning fires" was true, but firing-into-an-unread-log carries none of the weight "so nothing is lost by suppressing the other check" needed it to carry. The gap between "the fact checks out" and "the argument that leans on the fact checks out" is exactly where I stopped short both times this session (once relaying the architect's claim unchecked, once verifying the claim but not what it was supposed to prove).

**How to apply:** When a specialist's pushback rests on a factual claim, after verifying the claim is true, ask one more question explicitly: *what is this fact being used to establish, and does it actually establish that?* ("A warning exists" does not establish "no severity information is lost" — those are different properties.) If the fact and the conclusion are answering different questions, the argument doesn't survive even though the premise checked out. This extends [[review_verify_external_state]] and [[review_verify_quoted_review_via_api]] one level further: verify the claim, then verify the inference drawn from the claim, separately.

**Extra scrutiny at the moment of concession specifically.** lucos-architect's framing of this, worth keeping verbatim: *"a concession is the moment a claim is least likely to be examined... the person conceding has stopped arguing, and the person receiving it has just been agreed with... nobody stress-tests the thing they've just won."* Apply the two-step check above hardest exactly when a specialist is retracting their own objection in my favor, not just when they're pushing back — that's precisely when scrutiny (mine and theirs) drops.

**The concrete question to ask at that moment:** *what argument are they conceding **to**, and is it actually stronger than the one they're giving up?* The architect's own failure here was conceding "the detector must not go blind during the condition it exists for" (strong) in exchange for "there's another log line" (weaker on its face) — one question would have caught that trade was backwards before it propagated.
